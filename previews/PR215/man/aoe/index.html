A/E Pulse-Shape Discrimination

The ratio A/E of the maximum current amplitude A of a detector pulse to its energy E separates single-site events (SSE) from multi-site events (MSE) and from some surface events. LegendSpecFits.jl fits the A/E distribution in the Compton continuum to normalize A/E as a function of energy, finds the A/E cut that keeps a chosen fraction of single-site events, and measures how many events of several Th-228 calibration lines and of the continuum survive that cut. A runnable end-to-end chain is given in the A/E tutorial; this page describes the concepts and the functions.

Event Classes and Reference Peaks

A neutrinoless double-beta decay deposits its energy mostly within a few millimeters, which produces a single current pulse with a large A/E. A photon that scatters several times in the detector deposits energy at separated positions, so the current pulses of the individual deposits are spread in time and A/E is smaller. Events close to the detector surface can have an A/E that is smaller or larger than the single-site value.

The Th-228 calibration spectrum provides samples of both classes:

FeatureEnergy (keV)Dominant event class
Double escape peak (DEP) of the 2614.5 keV line1592.5single-site; used as a proxy for signal events
Single escape peak (SEP)2103.5multi-site
Full energy peak (FEP)2614.5multi-site
Compton continuumany, e.g. around $Q_{\beta\beta}$ = 2039.06 keVmixture of both classes

In the DEP both 511 keV annihilation photons escape the detector, so the remaining energy is deposited locally. The fraction of DEP events that pass an A/E cut therefore estimates the signal efficiency of the cut. The survival fractions of the SEP, FEP, and the continuum around $Q_{\beta\beta}$ quantify how strongly the cut rejects background-like events.

A/E Normalization

The raw A/E value depends on the detector and on the pulse-processing settings. A first normalization divides A/E by the position of the single-site peak, so that this peak is located near 1. cut_single_peak returns this position as the field max, together with the bounds low and high of a window around the peak:

cuts = cut_single_peak(aoe, 0.0, quantile(filter(isfinite, aoe), 0.99); n_bins = -1)
aoe_normalized = aoe ./ cuts.max
aoe_expression = "<property function of A/E> / $(cuts.max)"

The fits described below determine the remaining dependence of the peak position and width on energy. After that correction, the A/E value is (A/E - μ(E)) / σ(E): the single-site peak is at 0 and has a width of 1.

Compton Bands

The fits use events from several narrow energy intervals ("Compton bands") of the continuum. generate_aoe_compton_bands takes the A/E values, the calibrated energies, the lower band edges compton_bands, and the common band width compton_window. For every band, it selects the events with energy between the band edge and the edge plus the width and with positive A/E, builds an A/E histogram, and estimates start values for the fit:

bands = generate_aoe_compton_bands(aoe, e_cal, compton_bands, compton_window)
(; peakhists, peakstats) = bands

The histogram range is restricted to the region around the single-site peak, and the bin width follows the Freedman-Diaconis rule. The returned NamedTuple also contains the histogram limits and simple estimates of the energy dependence of the peak position and width. aoe and e_cal must have the same length. The estimate of the peak width uses all bands from the 20th on, so at least 20 bands are required; with fewer bands the function throws an ArgumentError.

A/E Peak Shape

In one Compton band, the A/E distribution is the sum of a Gaussian single-site peak and a multi-site background that extends toward lower A/E. aoe_compton_peakshape implements the model

\[f(x) = n \, \mathcal{N}(x;\, \mu, \sigma) + B \, \mathrm{EMG}(-x;\, -\mu, \sigma, \delta)\,,\]

where $x$ is the A/E value, $\mathcal{N}(x; \mu, \sigma) = \exp\!\left(-(x-\mu)^2 / (2\sigma^2)\right) / (\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$ is the Gaussian density with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, and $n$ is the number of events in the single-site peak. The background term is an exponentially modified Gaussian (EMG), the density of the sum of a Gaussian random variable with mean $\mu_g$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ and an exponentially distributed variable with scale $\theta$:

\[\mathrm{EMG}(x;\, \mu_g, \sigma, \theta) = \frac{1}{2\theta}\, \exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta^2} - \frac{x-\mu_g}{\theta}\right) \mathrm{erfc}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\theta} - \frac{x-\mu_g}{\sigma}\right)\right)\,,\]

where $\mathrm{erfc}$ is the complementary error function. The argument $-x$ and the mean $-\mu$ mirror the EMG, so the background tail extends toward lower A/E. $B$ is the number of events in the background component and $\delta$ is the scale of its tail, in units of A/E. The background shares $\mu$ and $\sigma$ with the signal peak. The signal and background parts can be evaluated separately with aoe_compton_signal_peakshape and aoe_compton_background_peakshape.

The fit function :aoe_two_bck adds a second EMG with its own mean $\mu_2$, width $\sigma_2$, and scale $\delta_2$ (two_emg_aoe_compton_peakshape):

\[f_2(x) = n \, \mathcal{N}(x;\, \mu, \sigma) + B \left[(1 - f_B)\, \mathrm{EMG}(-x;\, -\mu, \sigma, \delta) + f_B\, \mathrm{EMG}(-x;\, -\mu_2, \sigma_2, \delta_2)\right]\,,\]

where $f_B$ is the fraction of the background in the second component (B2f in the fit results). The default fit function is :aoe_one_bck with the parameters μ, σ, n, B, δ; :aoe_two_bck has the additional parameters μ2, σ2, B2f, δ2. Other peak shapes are described in Peak Shapes.

Fitting Single Bands

fit_aoe_compton fits every band histogram with a binned maximum-likelihood fit (Poisson likelihood per bin). The keyword fit_func selects the peak shape and uncertainty = true computes parameter uncertainties from the Hessian of the likelihood. Without uncertainties, the parameter errors are NaN, and the later correction fit cannot use the bands. The bands are fitted via

result_fit, report_fit = fit_aoe_compton(peakhists, peakstats, compton_bands; uncertainty = true)

Both return values are ordered dictionaries keyed by the lower band edge. Each entry of result_fit contains the fitted parameters as Measurements (μ, σ, n, B, δ, and the second-background parameters for :aoe_two_bck), the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the peak, the name fit_func, and a gof field with the p-value, $\chi^2$, degrees of freedom, covariance matrix, normalized residuals, and a converged flag. The entries of report_fit hold the fit values, the histogram, the fit function and its components, and the residuals for plotting with lplot from LegendMakie. If the fit of a band throws an error, the function logs a warning and continues; building the result dictionary then fails because that band has no result.

The dataflow keeps the bands with gof.converged and a p-value above a configured threshold and uses their $\mu$ and $\sigma$ in the next step:

good = [result_fit[b].gof.converged && result_fit[b].gof.pvalue >= p_value_cut for b in compton_bands]
compton_bands_qc = compton_bands[good]
μ = [result_fit[b].μ for b in compton_bands_qc]
σ = [result_fit[b].σ for b in compton_bands_qc]

Energy Dependence of the Peak

The position and width of the single-site peak depend on energy. fit_aoe_corrections fits the band results $\mu_i$ and $\sigma_i$ at the band energies $E_i$ with the models

\[\mu(E) = \mu_A + \mu_B\, E\,, \qquad \sigma(E) = \sqrt{\sigma_A^2 + \frac{\sigma_B^2}{E^2}}\,,\]

where $\mu_A$ and $\sigma_A$ are dimensionless offsets, $\mu_B$ is a slope in units of 1/keV, and $\sigma_B$ is a term in units of keV that dominates at low energy. The function uses a weighted least-squares fit (chi2fit) and skips bands whose fit parameter uncertainties are zero or NaN.

It returns a (result, report) pair. result.µ_compton and result.σ_compton contain the fit parameters par (with units), the goodness of fit, and the expression strings func; result.compton_bands.e contains the band energies used. result.func is the normalization as an expression string that can be evaluated with a property function on a table of hits, with the placeholders replaced by the keywords aoe_expression and e_expression:

using LegendSpecFits, Random, Unitful, Measurements

Random.seed!(2)
bands = collect((1000:100:1900)u"keV")
E = ustrip.(bands)
μ = measurement.(0.99 .+ 1e-5 .* E .+ 2e-4 .* randn(length(E)), 3e-4)
σ = measurement.(sqrt.(0.012^2 .+ 11.0^2 ./ E .^ 2) .+ 1e-4 .* randn(length(E)), 3e-4)

result, report = fit_aoe_corrections(bands, μ, σ; aoe_expression = "aoe", e_expression = "e")
println(result.func)
((aoe) - (0.9897005044371148 + (e) * 1.0161484951820593e-5keV^-1) ) / (sqrt( (0.01210161600890702)^2 + (10.803202882730744keV)^2 / (e)^2 ))

The value of the model at the energy of an event is subtracted from its A/E and the difference is divided by $\sigma(E)$, so the normalized A/E has mean 0 and standard deviation 1 for single-site events at any energy. The reports report.report_μ and report.report_σ hold the data and the fit curves for plotting.

Combined Fit

fit_aoe_compton_combined replaces the independent fits of the bands by a single fit of all bands in which the peak position and width follow the models above. The four parameters $\mu_A$, $\mu_B$, $\sigma_A$, $\sigma_B$ are free in the likelihood, with Gaussian priors centered on the result of fit_aoe_corrections (passed as result_corrections); the other parameters of each band are fitted for every trial value. The result has the same fields as the result of fit_aoe_corrections (µ_compton, σ_compton, compton_bands, func), so result.func can be used as a replacement, and additionally contains the parameters μA, μB, σA, σB and a gof field. The combined fit is called via

result_combined, report_combined = fit_aoe_compton_combined(
    peakhists, peakstats, compton_bands_qc, result_correction;
    aoe_expression, e_expression, uncertainty = true,
)

The dataflow runs this fit when the use_combined_fit option is set.

A/E Charge-Trapping Correction

The A/E of single-site events can also depend on the drift time of the charges, for example because of charge trapping. ctc_aoe removes a polynomial dependence on a drift-time parameter, by default qdrift / e (the digital signal processing (DSP) parameter qdrift divided by the energy). It uses the A/E that is already normalized with result_correction.func and is called via

result_ctc, report_ctc = LegendSpecFits.ctc_aoe(
    aoe_corrected, e_cal, qdrift_e, compton_bands_qc;
    aoe_expression = result_correction.func, qdrift_expression = "qdrift / e",
)

Only events in the Compton bands (each band extends 20 keV above its lower edge) and with an A/E in [peak - window[1], peak + window[2]] enter the fit. The defaults are peak = 0 (the normalized single-site position) and window = (50, 8). With $q$ the drift-time parameter and $c_i$ the coefficients, the corrected value is

\[a_\mathrm{ctc} = a + \sum_{i=1}^{p} c_i\, q^i\,,\]

where $a$ is the normalized A/E and $p$ is the polynomial order (pol_order, default 1). The coefficients are found with a bounded derivative-free optimizer (BOBYQA from NLopt) that minimizes the standard deviation of a right-sided truncated Gaussian fit to $a_\mathrm{ctc}$ around the single-site peak. A final truncated Gaussian fit then re-centers the corrected A/E to 0 and scales its width to 1. The result contains the expression string func of the full correction, the coefficients fct, the widths before and after the correction, the fit results before, after, and after_norm, and the flag converged; a warning is logged if the optimizer does not converge. The Compton-band peak shape is fitted with fit_func = :aoe_two_bck by default. The correction is optional in the dataflow (option apply_ctc), and result_ctc.func then replaces result_correction.func as the final A/E expression.

Low and High Cuts

get_low_aoe_cut determines the lower A/E threshold such that a chosen fraction dep_sf (default 0.9) of the DEP events survives. It selects the events around the DEP (window = [12.0, 10.0]u"keV" below and above dep), fits the DEP peak without any cut, and searches for the threshold with the bisection method on cut_search_interval until the survival fraction obtained from a combined fit of the surviving and the rejected events equals dep_sf within the relative tolerance rtol. With fixed_position = true, the peak centroid is fixed to the value dep in these fits. The cut is determined via

result_cut, report_cut = get_low_aoe_cut(aoe, e_cal; dep = 1592.53u"keV", dep_sf = 0.9)
result_cut.lowcut, result_cut.highcut

The result is a NamedTuple with the fields lowcut (a Measurement whose uncertainty is set by rtol), highcut, n0, nsf (the number of DEP events before and after the cut), and sf (survival fraction in percent). The upper cut is not determined by a fit: highcut is the value of the keyword sigma_high_sided in the unit of the A/E input and is Inf by default, i.e., no upper cut. For normalized A/E, the value is in units of the single-site width $\sigma$. report_cut contains the energy histograms before the cut, after the low cut, and after both cuts for plotting with lplot. An event passes the cuts if lowcut < aoe < highcut.

Survival Fractions

The survival fraction of an energy peak is the fraction of the peak events that remain after an event selection. get_peaks_survival_fractions evaluates it for several peaks. For each peak, it fits the peak before the cut, and then fits the energy histograms of surviving and rejected events simultaneously with a shared peak shape, so the survival fraction refers to the peak signal without the underlying background. The survival fractions of the Th-228 lines are obtained via

peaks = [583.19, 1592.53, 1620.5, 2103.53, 2614.51]u"keV"   # Th-228 lines
names = [:Tl208FEP_583, :Tl208DEP, :Bi212FEP, :Tl208SEP, :Tl208FEP]
windows_left  = [15.0, 12.0, 10.0, 25.0, 20.0]u"keV"                   # window below each peak
windows_right = [15.0, 10.0, 10.0, 25.0, 20.0]u"keV"                   # window above each peak

result_peaks, report_peaks = get_peaks_survival_fractions(
    aoe, e_cal, peaks, names, windows_left, windows_right;
    low_cut = result_cut.lowcut, high_cut = result_cut.highcut,
    uncertainty = true, bin_width_window = 2.0u"keV",
)
result_peaks[:Tl208SEP].sf   # survival fraction in percent, with uncertainty

The function accepts either an event selection (a Vector{Bool} survival_flag, passed after the windows) or a cut parameter (here A/E) with the keywords low_cut, high_cut, and selection, an additional Boolean mask. The cut bounds are exclusive. The energies, windows, and bin width must share one floating-point element type, e.g., Float64 keV. The windows can be given as a vector of (left, right) tuples or as two vectors of widths. The keyword fit_funcs selects the peak shape of each peak (default :gamma_def, see Peak Shapes). The result is an ordered dictionary keyed by the peak names; each entry contains peak, n_before, n_after, sf (percent), fit_func, and gof. The peaks are fitted in parallel threads.

get_peak_survival_fraction performs the same computation for one peak and also accepts histograms of all, surviving, and rejected events. get_continuum_survival_fraction counts the events in a window center ± window before and after the selection; it is used for the continuum around $Q_{\beta\beta}$, where no peak is fitted. Its uncertainty is the binomial uncertainty of the ratio.

using LegendSpecFits, Random, Distributions, Unitful

Random.seed!(1)
n = 60_000
e = (2039.06 .+ 200 .* (rand(n) .- 0.5))u"keV"
aoe = randn(n)                                  # normalized A/E: single-site peak at 0, width 1
tail = rand(n) .< 0.5
aoe[tail] .-= rand(Exponential(8.0), count(tail))   # half of the events have a multi-site tail

result, report = get_continuum_survival_fraction(aoe, e, 2039.06u"keV", 25.0u"keV"; low_cut = -1.5)
result.sf
54.93 ± 0.41 %

Order of the A/E Steps

The production dataflow chains the functions in this order for every detector and A/E definition:

  1. Normalize A/E to the single-site peak with cut_single_peak and cuts.max.
  2. Build the bands with generate_aoe_compton_bands and fit them with fit_aoe_compton (uncertainty = true).
  3. Keep the bands that converged and have a sufficient p-value.
  4. Determine the energy dependence with fit_aoe_corrections, or with fit_aoe_compton_combined if the combined fit is enabled. The func string of the result is the normalization.
  5. Optionally apply ctc_aoe, whose func becomes the final A/E expression.
  6. Compute the cut with get_low_aoe_cut on the final A/E.
  7. Evaluate the survival fractions of the calibration peaks with get_peaks_survival_fractions for the low cut alone and for the low and high cut together, and of the $Q_{\beta\beta}$ continuum with get_continuum_survival_fraction.

An earlier optimization step chooses the pulse-processing window length that minimizes the SEP survival fraction at fixed DEP survival fraction with fit_sf_wl; see Filter Optimization. The survival fractions of the A/E cut are combined with those of the LQ cut (LQ Cut) in the PSD efficiency step.
