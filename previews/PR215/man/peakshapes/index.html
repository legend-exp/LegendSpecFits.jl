Peak Shapes and Likelihoods

LegendSpecFits.jl describes a gamma line in an energy spectrum with a parametric peak shape: a Gaussian signal, optional exponential tails and a smooth background. The shape is fitted to a histogram by maximizing a binned Poisson likelihood. This page lists the shape functions, the available fit_func options, the likelihood, the priors that set the start values, and the generic single-peak fits that do not use the gamma peak shape.

Gamma Peak Shape

gamma_peakshape evaluates the sum of four components, each available as a separate function:

\[f(x) = \underbrace{n\,(1 - f_\mathrm{L} - f_\mathrm{H})\,\mathcal{N}(x;\mu,\sigma)}_{\text{signal}} + \underbrace{n\,f_\mathrm{L}\,\mathcal{E}(-x;-\mu,\sigma,\theta_\mathrm{L})}_{\text{low-energy tail}} + \underbrace{n\,f_\mathrm{H}\,\mathcal{E}(x;\mu,\sigma,\theta_\mathrm{H})}_{\text{high-energy tail}} + \underbrace{A\,S(x;\mu,\sigma) + b\,e^{-d\,(x - x_\mathrm{c})} + s\,(x - x_\mathrm{c})}_{\text{background}}\,,\]

where

  • \[x\]

    is the energy (or any other horizontal axis of the histogram) and $f(x)$ is a density in counts per unit of $x$,

  • \[\mathcal{N}(x;\mu,\sigma)\]

    is the normal density with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ (LegendSpecFits.gauss_pdf),

  • \[\mathcal{E}(x;\mu,\sigma,\theta)\]

    is the density of an exponentially modified Gaussian with exponential scale $\theta$ (LegendSpecFits.ex_gauss_pdf),

    \[\mathcal{E}(x;\mu,\sigma,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\theta}\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta^2} - \frac{x-\mu}{\theta}\right)\mathrm{erfc}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\theta} - \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)\right)\,.\]

    Evaluating it at $-x$ with mean $-\mu$ mirrors the tail toward lower energies,

  • \[n\]

    is the number of counts in the signal and the tails together,

  • \[f_\mathrm{L}\]

    and $f_\mathrm{H}$ are the fractions of $n$ in the low- and high-energy tail, and $\theta_\mathrm{L} = w_\mathrm{L}\,\mu$ and $\theta_\mathrm{H} = w_\mathrm{H}\,\mu$ are their exponential scales, given by the relative widths $w_\mathrm{L}$ and $w_\mathrm{H}$,

  • \[S(x;\mu,\sigma) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\left((x-\mu)/(\sqrt{2}\,\sigma)\right)\]

    is a step that falls from 1 to 0 across $\mu$ with the resolution $\sigma$ (LegendSpecFits.step_gauss), and $A$ is its amplitude. It models the higher background below the peak that comes from Compton-scattered photons of the same line,

  • \[b\]

    is the flat background level, $d$ the decay constant of an exponential background, $s$ the slope of a linear background and $x_\mathrm{c}$ the reference position of the last two terms. By default, $x_\mathrm{c} = \mu$.

The parameters carry the following names in function arguments and in fit results:

SymbolNameMeaning
$\mu$, $\sigma$μ, σposition and standard deviation of the Gaussian
$n$ncounts in signal and tails
$f_\mathrm{L}$, $w_\mathrm{L}$skew_fraction, skew_widthlow-energy tail
$f_\mathrm{H}$, $w_\mathrm{H}$skew_fraction_highE, skew_width_highEhigh-energy tail
$A$step_amplitudeamplitude of the step background
$b$backgroundflat background
$s$background_slopeslope of a linear background
$d$background_expdecay constant of an exponential background

The components are available individually as signal_peakshape, lowEtail_peakshape, highEtail_peakshape and background_peakshape. The low-energy tail is the most important one for HPGe detectors: incomplete charge collection moves a small fraction of the events of a line to lower energies.

Fit Functions

The fit_func keyword of fit_single_peak_th228, fit_peaks and the functions built on them selects which terms of $f(x)$ are active. Each option corresponds to a function f(x, v) of the energy and a NamedTuple v of parameters. LegendSpecFits.get_th228_fit_functions returns all of them as a NamedTuple.

fit_funcActive termsFitted parameters
:gamma_def (default)signal, low-energy tail, flat and step backgroundμ σ n skew_fraction skew_width background step_amplitude
:gamma_tails:gamma_def plus high-energy tail:gamma_def and skew_fraction_highE skew_width_highE
:gamma_bckSlope:gamma_def plus linear background:gamma_def and background_slope
:gamma_bckExp:gamma_def plus exponential background:gamma_def and background_exp
:gamma_bckFlat:gamma_def without step backgroundμ σ n skew_fraction skew_width background
:gamma_tails_bckFlat:gamma_tails without step background:gamma_bckFlat and skew_fraction_highE skew_width_highE
:gamma_minimalGaussian signal and flat backgroundμ σ n background

get_th228_fit_functions also contains gamma_sigWithTail (signal and low-energy tail only). It is used to compute the FWHM of the fitted peak and is not a fit_func option.

The FWHM reported by the fit is the width of signal plus low-energy tail at half of its maximum (LegendSpecFits.estimate_fwhm), so the tail widens the reported FWHM compared to $2\sqrt{2\ln 2}\,\sigma$. The peak position that follows from the same shape is available as peak_centroid, the mean of the Gaussian shifted by the tails.

LegendSpecFits.peakshape_components returns, for a fit_func and a parameter set, the component functions together with labels, colors and line styles. The plot recipes of LegendMakie use it for the component curves of a fit report.

The following code evaluates the :gamma_def shape for a set of parameters and draws its components:

using LegendSpecFits, CairoMakie

v = (μ = 2614.5, σ = 1.8, n = 2.0e4, skew_fraction = 0.05, skew_width = 0.002,
     background = 25.0, step_amplitude = 3.0)
f_gamma_def = LegendSpecFits.get_th228_fit_functions().gamma_def
components = LegendSpecFits.peakshape_components(:gamma_def, v)

x = range(2590, 2635, length = 400)
fig = Figure(size = (700, 350))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "Energy (keV)", ylabel = "Counts / keV", yscale = log10)
lines!(ax, x, f_gamma_def.(x, Ref(v)); color = :black, label = "Sum")
for k in keys(components.funcs)
    lines!(ax, x, components.funcs[k].(x); color = components.colors[k],
        linestyle = components.linestyles[k], label = components.labels[k])
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lt)
fig

The expected counts in a bin of width $\Delta x$ are $f(x)\,\Delta x$ for $x$ at the bin center.

Besides the gamma peak, the package defines the shapes used for A/E distributions (aoe_compton_peakshape and its components, two_emg_aoe_compton_peakshape), the sum of two Gaussians double_gaussian, the energy dependence of the A/E resolution ex_step_gauss, and the generic linear_function and exponential_decay. The A/E shapes are used in A/E Pulse-Shape Discrimination.

Binned Poisson Likelihood

A histogram h with bin centers $x_i$, bin widths $\Delta x_i$ and counts $k_i$ is compared with a model density $f$ by the Poisson log-likelihood

\[\ln L(\vec{p}) = \sum_i \ln \mathrm{Pois}\!\left(k_i;\, \lambda_i\right), \qquad \lambda_i = \Delta x_i\, f(x_i; \vec{p})\,,\]

where $\vec{p}$ are the model parameters and $\lambda_i$ is the expected count in bin $i$. hist_loglike computes this sum for a function f_fit(x) of the energy. The model is evaluated at the bin centers only (midpoint rule), so the bin width must be small compared to the peak width $\sigma$.

fit_single_peak_th228 maximizes $\ln L$ with the L-BFGS-B algorithm. The uncertainties of the parameters are the square roots of the diagonal of the inverse Hessian matrix of $-\ln L$ at the maximum, computed with automatic differentiation. If this covariance matrix is not positive definite, it is replaced by the nearest positive-definite matrix. The goodness of fit uses the Poisson deviance (see Cuts and Goodness of Fit).

Priors and Start Values

Each fit parameter has a prior distribution. The prior does not enter the objective function: the fit is a maximum-likelihood fit, not a maximum a posteriori fit. The priors serve two purposes:

  1. They define a transformation of the parameters to unconstrained, standard-normal coordinates in which the optimizer runs. This keeps parameters inside their allowed ranges, for example $\sigma > 0$.
  2. The mean of each prior is the start value of the optimizer. This is why these distributions are called pseudo-priors in the code.

The default pseudo-priors depend on the initial peak statistics ps, a NamedTuple that estimate_single_peak_stats computes from the histogram. For calib_type = :th228 it contains the peak position, FWHM and standard deviation, the number of counts in the peak, the bin width, and the mean and standard deviation of the background and of the background step. The defaults are:

  • μ: normal distribution around the estimated position with a standard deviation of 20 % of the estimated peak_sigma,
  • σ, n, background, step_amplitude: Weibull distributions (see below) centered on the estimated values,
  • skew_fraction, skew_fraction_highE: Weibull distributions with a median of 0.002 (before truncation), truncated to at most 0.5 and 0.1,
  • skew_width, skew_width_highE: Weibull distributions with a median of the estimated relative peak width,
  • background_slope: zero if the mean background is below 5 counts per bin, otherwise a truncated normal distribution of non-positive slopes,
  • background_exp: Weibull distribution with a median of 0.03.

weibull_from_mx builds a Weibull distribution from its median m and a second point x that is the p_x-quantile, 68.27 % by default. Weibull distributions are positive and skewed, which suits quantities that have a natural scale but no sharp upper bound:

using Distributions

d = weibull_from_mx(1.0, 1.5)
(median(d), cdf(d, 1.5))
(1.0000000019548827, 0.6826999994650859)

With low_e_tail = false, the tail fractions are fixed to zero. With fixed_position = true, μ is fixed to the estimated position. The pseudo_prior keyword replaces individual default priors. Its value is a NamedTupleDist from ValueShapes.jl, and its keys must be parameters of the chosen fit_func. A ConstValueDist fixes a parameter:

using Distributions, ValueShapes

pseudo_prior = NamedTupleDist(
    σ = Normal(2.0, 0.2),             # replaces the default prior of σ
    skew_fraction = ConstValueDist(0.0),  # fixes the parameter
)
result, report = fit_single_peak_th228(h, ps; pseudo_prior)

Fitting a Single Peak

fit_single_peak_th228 takes a histogram and the peak statistics and returns the usual (result, report) pair. result holds the fitted parameters as Measurements, the fwhm and centroid of the peak, the used fit_func and the goodness of fit gof. report holds everything needed to plot the fit with lplot from LegendMakie. fit_peaks runs the same fit for a list of lines in parallel; it is described with the energy calibration.

The example generates a 2614.5 keV line with a low-energy tail on a flat background, estimates the start values and fits :gamma_def:

using Random, StatsBase, Measurements

Random.seed!(1)
E = 2614.5
energies = vcat(
    rand(Normal(E, 1.8), 20_000),                         # signal
    E .- rand(Exponential(4.0), 1_500) .+ 1.8 .* randn(1_500),  # low-energy tail
    rand(Uniform(E - 40, E + 40), 4_000),                 # flat background
)
h = fit(Histogram, energies, (E - 30):0.5:(E + 30))
ps = estimate_single_peak_stats(h)
result, report = fit_single_peak_th228(h, ps; fit_func = :gamma_def)
(μ = result.μ, σ = result.σ, fwhm = result.fwhm, pvalue = result.gof.pvalue)
(μ = 2614.495 ± 0.019, σ = 1.823 ± 0.013, fwhm = 4.349 ± 0.028, pvalue = 0.390627659912725)

The generating values are $\mu = 2614.5$ and $\sigma = 1.8$, which gives a FWHM of 4.24 for the Gaussian alone; the fitted FWHM is larger because it includes the low-energy tail. The report is plotted with LegendMakie:

using LegendMakie

lplot(report; title = "Synthetic 2614.5 keV line")

The upper panel shows the histogram, the best fit and its components on a logarithmic scale; the lower panel shows the normalized residuals.

Generic Single Fits

Several fit functions do not use the gamma peak shape. They fit a Gaussian to a single distribution, for example the distribution of a pulse-shape parameter, a baseline noise or a trigger threshold. They take the data and cuts, accept uncertainty = true and return (result, report); fit_half_centered_trunc_gauss takes the fixed mean as an additional argument before cuts. The cuts argument is the NamedTuple (low, high, max) that cut_single_peak returns. A NaN bound is replaced by the edge of the data. Data with a Unitful unit keep their unit in the results.

FunctionDataModelFitted parameters
fit_single_trunc_gaussvaluesGaussian truncated to [cuts.low, cuts.high], unbinned likelihoodμ σ
fit_half_trunc_gaussvaluesone half of a Gaussian: right of cuts.max or, with left = true, left of itμ σ
fit_half_centered_trunc_gaussvaluesone half of a Gaussian with fixed mean μσ
fit_binned_trunc_gausshistogramas fit_single_trunc_gauss, binned Poisson likelihood with amplitudeμ σ n
fit_binned_half_trunc_gausshistogramas fit_half_trunc_gauss, binned Poisson likelihood with amplitudeμ σ n
fit_binned_double_gausshistogramsum of two Gaussians (double_gaussian)μ1 σ1 n1 μ2 σ2 n2

The half-Gaussian fits use only the side of a peak that is not distorted by a tail or by a second population. The truncation to cuts excludes the background far from the peak.

A typical use is a window cut: the example below finds the peak region of a sample with cut_single_peak and fits the truncated Gaussian to it:

Random.seed!(2)
x = rand(Normal(5.0, 0.5), 5_000)
cuts = cut_single_peak(x, 0.0, 10.0)
result_gauss, report_gauss = fit_single_trunc_gauss(x, cuts)
(μ = result_gauss.μ, σ = result_gauss.σ)
(μ = 5.024 ± 0.017, σ = 0.543 ± 0.03)

report_gauss contains the fitted density f_fit and the histogram h normalized to a probability density, and is plotted with lplot(report_gauss).

Least-Squares Fits

chi2fit fits a model f(x, p...) to points $(x_i, y_i)$ with uncertainties by minimizing

\[\chi^2(\vec{p}) = \sum_i \frac{\left(y_i - f(x_i;\vec{p})\right)^2}{\sigma_{y,i}^2 + \left(f'(x_i;\vec{p})\,\sigma_{x,i}\right)^2} + \sum_j \frac{(p_j - m_j)^2}{s_j^2}\,,\]

where $\sigma_{x,i}$ and $\sigma_{y,i}$ are the uncertainties of the points and $f'$ is the derivative of the model with respect to $x$, computed by automatic differentiation. The second sum contains optional Gaussian pull terms with mean $m_j$ and standard deviation $s_j$ for selected parameters, given by the pull_t keyword. The covariance matrix of the parameters is $2H^{-1}$ with $H$ the Hessian matrix of $\chi^2$ at the minimum. fit_calibration and fit_fwhm are built on this function. The input points are Reals or Measurements; with Measurements, the uncertainties are taken from the input.

The example fits a straight line with the polynomial shortcut chi2fit(n_poly, x, y, yerr):

Random.seed!(3)
xs = collect(1.0:10.0)
yerr = fill(0.1, 10)
ys = 2 .* xs .+ 1 .+ yerr .* randn(10)
result_chi2, report_chi2 = chi2fit(1, xs, ys, yerr)
(par = result_chi2.par, pvalue = result_chi2.gof.pvalue)
(par = Measurements.Measurement{Float64}[1.055 ± 0.068, 1.987 ± 0.011], pvalue = 0.5634729379103904)

The parameters par are ordered from the constant term upward, so par[1] is the intercept and par[2] the slope (see PolCalFunc). With correlated = true (the default) the parameters share their correlations, so that a derived quantity such as report_chi2.f_fit(5.0) has the uncertainty that follows from the full covariance matrix.
